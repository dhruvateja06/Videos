## SLIDE 3 — Callback to Episode 1

> Quick refresher, in one sentence. Last episode we said: AI is software that learns the *pattern* from examples, instead of being told the *rules*.
> ▸
> Rules versus patterns. That stays true today. We're not throwing it away. We're just zooming in — because inside that "pattern" world, there are layers. Four of them. And those four layers are the four words people keep mixing up.

## SLIDE 4 — The big reveal: they're nested

> Here's the move that fixes everything. These four words are NOT four separate things.
> ▸
> They are four *nested boxes.* One inside the other. Like Russian dolls.
> AI is the biggest box. Inside it sits Machine Learning. Inside that sits Deep Learning. And inside that sits Generative AI.
> ▸
> Every Generative AI is Deep Learning. Every Deep Learning is Machine Learning. Every Machine Learning is AI. But not the other way around. Get this picture in your head, and the rest of this video — and honestly, the rest of this series — becomes easy.

---

## SLIDE 5 — Box 1: AI (the outer box)

> Box one. The biggest one. **AI — Artificial Intelligence.**
> ▸
> The simplest way to think about AI is: any time a computer does something that *feels* like a human is doing it.
> ▸
> That's a huge umbrella. It includes the kind of AI from Episode 1 — the Swiggy ETA, the learning-from-data kind. But it ALSO includes really old, simple stuff. The chess program that beat Kasparov in 1997 was AI. Your washing machine's "fuzzy logic" setting from twenty years ago is technically AI. A pinball game where the ball follows you around — that's AI.
> ▸
> So AI is the *category.* The whole field. Anything that smells like machine intelligence. Now let's go one box smaller, because that's where things actually got interesting.

---

## SLIDE 6 — Box 2: Machine Learning

> Box two. Inside AI, the bigger, more important room: **Machine Learning.**
> ▸
> Machine Learning is the part of AI we explained in Episode 1. It's AI that learns from examples instead of following hand-written rules.
> ▸
> This is the box where the real revolution happened in the last fifteen years. Every time you hear "trained a model" — that's Machine Learning. Every time you hear "the more data it sees, the better it gets" — Machine Learning. The Swiggy line drawing through the cloud of dots from Episode 1? That was Machine Learning.
> ▸
> So now you can be precise: not all AI is Machine Learning. The chess program from 1997 was AI but not ML — it followed handwritten rules. But almost everything new and exciting in the last decade *is* ML.

---

## SLIDE 7 — Real example: UPI fraud detection

> Quick real-world anchor. Open your UPI app and try to send fifty thousand rupees to a number you've never paid before, at 3 AM, from a city you don't normally use. Watch what happens.
> ▸
> The app stops you. It asks for an extra check. Sometimes it just blocks the payment outright.
> ▸
> How? Nobody at NPCI wrote a rule that says "if amount over fifty thousand AND time after 2 AM AND new recipient AND unusual location THEN flag." They couldn't possibly write rules for every fraud pattern.
> ▸
> Instead, an ML model learned from millions of real transactions — the legitimate ones and the fraudulent ones — what fishy patterns look like. It draws an invisible line between "normal Dhruv" and "not normal Dhruv." That's Machine Learning, sitting in your phone, saving you money every single day.

---

## SLIDE 8 — Box 3: Deep Learning

> Box three. Now we go one level deeper, literally. **Deep Learning.**
> ▸
> Deep Learning is Machine Learning that uses a specific tool to find the pattern: a **neural network.**
> ▸
> A neural network is — for now, don't worry — just a giant web of tiny calculators connected to each other in layers. Think of it like a brain made of math, with many, many stacked layers of processing.
> ▸
> "Old-school" Machine Learning would draw a single line through the dots. Deep Learning can draw lines through patterns so wildly complicated — recognizing your face, your voice, a tumor on an X-ray — that traditional ML simply cannot keep up.
> ▸
> So Deep Learning is a *kind* of Machine Learning. The specific kind that runs on neural networks. We're going to spend an entire module on neural networks later in this season — for today, just remember: deep learning = ML, but with a brain-like web of layered math.

---

## SLIDE 9 — Why is it called "deep"?

> Quick aside, because everyone asks: why "deep"?
> ▸
> Because of those layers. A simple neural network has two or three layers stacked on top of each other. A "deep" neural network can have a hundred. A thousand. ChatGPT runs on networks with billions of connections across dozens of layers.
> ▸
> "Deep" doesn't mean "smart" or "philosophical." It literally just means "many layers stacked." That's it. That's the whole name.

---

## SLIDE 10 — Real example: your phone camera

> Real-world anchor for Deep Learning. Take your phone right now and open the camera. Point it at a face. Watch the little yellow box snap around the face. Switch to portrait mode — watch the background go beautifully blurred while the face stays sharp.
> ▸
> How? Your phone is, in real-time, running a Deep Learning model. A neural network with millions of parameters, trained on millions of photos, that knows what "a face" looks like and what "background" looks like.
> ▸
> Same technology helps Karnataka farmers detect crop disease by pointing a phone at a leaf. Same technology lets a radiologist's software spot a tumor a human might miss. That's Deep Learning. It's already in your pocket.

---

## SLIDE 11 — Box 4: Generative AI

> Box four. The smallest, newest, loudest one. **Generative AI.**
> ▸
> Generative AI is Deep Learning that does one specific, mind-bending thing: it *creates* new stuff. New text. New images. New code. New music. New video.
> ▸
> Up until 2022, almost every ML model in the world was a *predictor* or a *classifier.* "What's the delivery time?" "Is this fraud, yes or no?" "What's in this photo?" The model picked an answer from a fixed menu.
> ▸
> Generative AI flipped that. Instead of picking, it *makes.* ChatGPT doesn't pick a sentence from a list — it writes you a brand new sentence, one word at a time. Image generators don't show you the closest matching photo — they paint a completely new image that has never existed before.
> ▸
> So: every Generative AI you've ever used is Deep Learning. Which means it's also Machine Learning. Which means it's also AI. The four boxes, nested.

---

## SLIDE 12 — Real example: ChatGPT, image generators

> Real-world anchor for Generative AI — and you've used this one a hundred times. ChatGPT. Gemini. Claude. Sora. Midjourney.
> ▸
> When you type "write me an email to my landlord," and a fully-formed, polite email appears on the screen — that email did not exist five seconds ago. Nobody wrote it. The model generated it, one word at a time, by predicting the most likely next word, billions of times.
> ▸
> When you type "Bollywood movie poster of a tiger in a Mumbai monsoon" and an image appears — that image is not from a database. It was painted by math, pixel by pixel, from scratch.
> ▸
> Generative AI is the box everyone is currently obsessed with — and rightly so, it's wild. But remember: it's the smallest box. There's still a giant world of ML and AI outside it doing the unglamorous, important work.

## SLIDE 13 — The full picture

> Let me put the whole thing together on one screen, because this is the picture I want burned into your brain.
> ▸
> Outermost box: **AI** — any time a computer feels intelligent. Includes old-school chess programs, includes everything else inside.
> ▸
> Inside it: **Machine Learning** — AI that learns from data instead of following rules. Your Swiggy ETA, UPI fraud detection.
> ▸
> Inside that: **Deep Learning** — Machine Learning powered by neural networks. Your phone camera, voice assistants, medical imaging.
> ▸
> Inside that: **Generative AI** — Deep Learning that creates new things. ChatGPT, image generators, AI video.
> ▸
> Four boxes. Four examples. One mental model. Now any time you read a headline using one of these words, you can ask the only question that matters — *which box?*
