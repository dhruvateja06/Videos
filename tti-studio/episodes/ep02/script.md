## SLIDE 2 — The everyday confusion

> Try this — just open LinkedIn right now. Scroll for thirty seconds. I'm serious.
> You will find someone — usually a founder, usually very confident — using all four of these words like they mean the same thing.
> ▸
> "We use AI to do X." "We trained a Machine Learning model." "Our Deep Learning system." "It's powered by Generative AI." Same product. Same paragraph.
> ▸
> And look — I'm not making fun. Even people who actually work in this stuff mix these words up. The problem is, when *you* hear them mixed up, you start to think they mean the same thing. They don't. They're not even on the same level. And the second I show you the right picture, you literally cannot un-see it.

---

## SLIDE 3 — Callback to Episode 1

> Quick refresher from last week, one sentence — that's all we need.
> ▸
> AI is software that learns the *pattern* from examples. Instead of being told the *rules.* Rules versus patterns. Yeah?
> We're not throwing that away today. We're zooming in. Because inside that "pattern" world, there are layers — different kinds of pattern-learners that do different things. And those layers are the four words people keep mixing up.

---

## SLIDE 4 — The big reveal: they're nested

> Okay. Here's the move that fixes this for you. Permanently. Watch.
> These four words — they are not four separate things.
> ▸
> They are four boxes. Nested. One inside the other. Like Russian dolls.
> AI is the big outer box. Inside it, smaller, is Machine Learning. Inside *that*, smaller still, is Deep Learning. And right in the middle, the smallest doll — Generative AI.
> ▸
> So — and this is the key — every Generative AI is also Deep Learning. Every Deep Learning is also Machine Learning. Every Machine Learning is also AI. The other way around? Not always. Get this picture in your head. Honestly, the rest of this video is just me filling in what's inside each box.

---

## SLIDE 5 — Box 1: AI (the outer box)

> Box one. The biggest one. AI — Artificial Intelligence.
> The simplest definition? Any time a computer does something that *feels* like a human is doing it. That's the whole thing.
> ▸
> So yes — the stuff from last episode, the Swiggy ETA, the learn-from-data kind — that's AI. But it also includes some really *old*, really *simple* stuff.
> The chess program that beat Kasparov in 1997 — AI. Your Gmail spam filter — AI. A pinball game where the ball seems to chase you — also, technically, AI.
> ▸
> So AI is the *category.* The whole field. Anything that smells like a computer being intelligent. Now let's go one box smaller — because that's where things actually got interesting.

---

## SLIDE 6 — Box 2: Machine Learning

> Box two. Inside AI, the much more interesting room — Machine Learning.
> Machine Learning is AI that learns from data. Instead of following handwritten rules. That's it.
> ▸
> This is the box where the actual revolution happened in the last fifteen years. Anytime you hear "we trained a model" — Machine Learning. The Swiggy line through the cloud of dots from last episode? That was ML.
> ▸
> So — important — not all AI is ML. The 1997 chess program followed rules, no learning. AI, yes — but not ML. But almost everything new and exciting in the last decade *is* Machine Learning.

---

## SLIDE 7 — UPI fraud (ML in your pocket)

> Quick real-world anchor — because abstract definitions are fine, but you'll remember the example.
> Try this. Try sending fifty thousand rupees over UPI. At 3 AM. To a number you've never paid before. From a city you don't normally use. Watch what happens.
> ▸
> Blocked. Or held for extra verification. Or the app outright refuses.
> How? Nobody at NPCI sat down and wrote the rule "if amount over fifty K and time after 2 AM and new recipient *and* unusual location then flag." They couldn't possibly write rules for every fraud pattern.
> ▸
> Instead — an ML model looked at *billions* of real transactions. The normal ones. The fraud ones. And it figured out, on its own, what fishy looks like. It draws an invisible line between "normal you" and "not-normal you." That's Machine Learning. Sitting in your phone. Saving your money. Right now.

---

## SLIDE 8 — Box 3: Deep Learning

> Box three. Going one level deeper, literally — Deep Learning.
> Deep Learning is Machine Learning that uses one very specific tool to find the pattern. A neural network.
> ▸
> Now, a neural network — and don't worry, we're doing a whole module on this later — is basically just a giant web of tiny calculators, wired up in layers. Think of it as a brain built out of math. Many, many layers of it.
> ▸
> Here's the difference that matters — classical Machine Learning would draw a single neat line through the dots. Deep Learning can find patterns so wildly complicated — your face in a crowd, your voice in noise, a tumor on an X-ray — that classical ML just can't keep up.
> Deep Learning *is* Machine Learning. The specific kind that runs on neural networks. That's the whole story.

---

## SLIDE 9 — Why is it called "deep"?

> Quick aside — because everyone secretly wonders this. Why "deep"?
> ▸
> Because of those layers. A simple neural network has two or three layers stacked. A "deep" one has a hundred. ChatGPT? Hundreds of layers, billions of connections.
> Deep doesn't mean *smart.* Doesn't mean *philosophical.* It literally just means... a lot of layers, stacked on top of each other. That's it. That's the whole name.

---

## SLIDE 10 — Phone camera (DL in your pocket)

> Real-world anchor for Deep Learning. Take your phone. Right now. Open the camera. Point it at a face.
> Watch that little yellow box snap around the face. Switch to portrait mode — and *bam*, the background goes beautifully blurred while the face stays sharp.
> ▸
> How? Your phone — the little device in your hand — is running, in real time, a Deep Learning model. A neural network with millions of parameters. Trained on millions of photos. It just *knows* what a face is.
> Same exact technology — different training data — helps farmers in Karnataka spot crop disease from a leaf photo. Helps radiologists catch tumors a human eye might miss. Same Deep Learning underneath.

---

## SLIDE 11 — Box 4: Generative AI

> Box four. The smallest. The newest. And right now — by *far* — the loudest one. Generative AI.
> Generative AI is Deep Learning that does one specific, mind-bending thing — it *creates* new stuff. New text. New images. New code. New music. New video.
> ▸
> Here's why this is wild. Up until about 2022, almost every ML model on Earth was a *predictor* or a *classifier.* "What's the delivery time?" "Is this fraud — yes or no?" "What's in this photo?" The model picked an answer from a fixed menu.
> Generative AI flipped that. Instead of *picking*, it *makes.* ChatGPT doesn't pick a sentence from a list — it writes you a brand new one. Word by word. Image generators don't search for a matching photo — they paint a new one. Pixel by pixel.
> Every Generative AI is Deep Learning. Which is Machine Learning. Which is AI. All four boxes — nested.

---

## SLIDE 12 — ChatGPT (GenAI in your pocket)

> Real-world anchor. And honestly, you don't need me to introduce this one — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Sora, Midjourney.
> You type "write me an email to my landlord about the leaking tap." A fully formed, polite, contextual email appears on the screen. That email *did not exist* five seconds ago. Nobody wrote it.
> ▸
> The model generated it. One word at a time. By predicting what word is most likely to come next. Billions of times.
> Type "Bollywood movie poster of a tiger in a Mumbai monsoon" — that image came from nowhere. It was painted by math. Pixel by pixel. By a Deep Learning model trained on hundreds of millions of images.
> That's GenAI. The smallest box. But everyone's obsessed with it right now — and honestly, fairly.

---

## SLIDE 13 — The full picture

> Okay — the whole map. On one screen. This is the picture I want burned into your brain.
> ▸
> Outermost box — AI. Any computer that feels intelligent. Includes the chess program from the nineties. Includes everything inside.
> Inside that — Machine Learning. AI that learned from data. UPI fraud detection. Swiggy's delivery times.
> Inside that — Deep Learning. ML powered by neural networks. Your phone camera. Voice assistants. Medical imaging.
> And the smallest one in the middle — Generative AI. Deep Learning that *creates* new things. ChatGPT. Image generators. AI video.
> Four boxes. Four examples. One mental model. That's the whole episode. You're done.

## SLIDE 14 — Your new habit

> Okay — your new habit. Starting today.
> Someone says "we're using AI to do X." That has told you... almost nothing. AI is the giant outer box. Includes a 1990s washing machine. Includes GPT-5. They are not the same thing.
> ▸
> So push back gently. Ask — which box? Is it Machine Learning, learning from data? Is it Deep Learning, neural networks? Is it Generative AI, actually making something new?
> Nine times out of ten — I promise — the person who said "AI" won't actually know. Now you do. And suddenly, you're the calmest, clearest person in the room.

## SLIDE 15 — Recap

> Lock it in. Four sentences. The whole video.
> ▸
> AI — any computer doing something intelligent. The whole field.
> ML — AI that learns from data, instead of being told the rules.
> Deep Learning — Machine Learning, but using a neural network.
> Generative AI — Deep Learning that creates new things. Text, images, code, video.
> Read those four lines back to yourself once a week, for a month. They'll be locked in for the rest of your career in tech.
