**[1 · HOOK]**
How does one app — Swiggy, Instagram, anything — handle millions of people at once and just… not crash? That's system design. And the fastest way to get it? Build a tiny app ourselves. Let's go.

**[2 · WHAT IS IT]**
So what even is system design? It's planning an app's parts — and how they connect — *before* you build it. Writing code is laying bricks. System design is the architect's blueprint. Today, you're the architect.

**[3 · MENTAL MODEL]**
Here's the secret that makes it click. Under the hood, almost every app is just three things. You — your phone — the client. The app, running on a computer somewhere — the server. And where everything's saved — the database. You ask, it answers: a request, and a response.

**[4 · THE 4 STEPS]**
To design any system, engineers follow the same four steps. Requirements. High-level design. Core components. Then scale it. They call it the system design life cycle.

**[5 · SCALE]**
Watch what happens at scale. Ten friends? One server's fine. But it's 9pm and ten million people open it at once — that one server melts. So: add more servers to share the load — that's scaling out. And keep the popular stuff nearby, in a cache.

**[6 · HLD vs LLD]**
You'll hear two terms everywhere. Drawing the boxes — that's high-level design, HLD. Zooming into one box to write its actual code — that's low-level design, LLD. Same craft, two zoom levels.

**[7 · REAL SCALE]**
And this isn't theory. Hotstar streamed the 2023 World Cup final to fifty-nine million people — at the same moment. Same request and response you just learned. Just scaled out, with a lot more boxes.

**[8 · OUTRO]**
And that's system design — you just did it. Decide the parts, connect them, keep it fast and standing as it grows. Follow along — we build the whole thing, one box at a time.
